# Carbon Actual Physical-World Interoperability Contract

**Status:** CANONICAL CROSS-CUTTING CAPABILITY CONTRACT  
**Authority:** HAPI World `CANON.md` + Carbon Actual operating-spine contracts

## Purpose

Carbon Actual is not limited to software screens. The same canonical objects, identity, authority, events, evidence, state, value and relationships may represent physical resources, machines, sensors, facilities, vehicles, robots and real-world interventions.

The physical-world layer is an interoperability boundary, not a new constitution.

## Canonical relationship

`physical object/process → canonical identity/context → capability → authority → physical action → observation → evidence → state → value`

Physical execution never bypasses the same authority and evidence rules used by digital execution.

## Representable physical participants

The ecosystem may represent:
- person;
- organization/institution;
- vehicle;
- machine;
- robot;
- building/facility;
- land/territory;
- inventory/material;
- agricultural/biological object;
- sensor;
- actuator;
- device;
- networked equipment;
- production line;
- shipment;
- energy resource;
- environmental observation;
- other future physical or biological entities.

Unknown future entity classes remain provisional until sufficient identity, capability, relationship, evidence and safety semantics exist.

## Sensors and observations

Sensor data is an observation/evidence source.

A sensor reading MUST retain where material:
- sensor/device identity;
- source/provenance;
- time;
- location/context;
- measurement/unit;
- calibration or quality metadata where available;
- transformation/processing history;
- confidence/verification state.

A sensor reading does not automatically become canonical truth.

## Actuators and physical actions

Actuation is consequential execution.

Before material actuation, the system MUST resolve:
- principal identity;
- applicable authority/delegation;
- capability;
- target/object;
- scope;
- safety constraints;
- operating conditions;
- policy;
- confirmation/approval where required.

An actuator's technical ability is not authority.

## Hardware and protocol neutrality

Physical integrations are replaceable adapters.

Possible protocols and interfaces include:
- IP/networked devices;
- Bluetooth;
- Wi-Fi;
- cellular;
- industrial fieldbus;
- message brokers;
- robotics APIs;
- telemetry streams;
- device gateways;
- local/offline capture;
- future machine protocols.

The specific protocol is not part of the constitutional model.

## Digital twins and simulations

A simulation or digital twin is a derived representation.

It MUST distinguish:
- observed physical state;
- inferred state;
- simulated future state;
- predicted outcome;
- planned action.

Simulation output cannot silently overwrite observed reality.

## Physical events

Examples include:
- machine started/stopped;
- sensor reading;
- maintenance performed;
- inspection performed;
- package scanned;
- vehicle departed/arrived;
- handoff accepted/rejected;
- production completed;
- asset transformed;
- environmental condition observed;
- safety incident;
- fault/anomaly;
- recovery action.

Events retain provenance and can feed continuity, Pulse, Value and audit flows.

## Safety and human oversight

Physical interventions may create safety, financial, environmental or legal consequences.

The system MUST fail closed where material authority, safety or evidence conditions are unresolved.

Emergency behavior, where domain law permits, remains explicitly bounded and auditable.

## Maintenance and lifecycle

Physical resources may carry:
- creation/commissioning;
- inspection;
- calibration;
- maintenance;
- repair;
- upgrade;
- replacement;
- suspension;
- quarantine;
- retirement;
- disposal/recovery;
- provenance and ownership/custody history.

Maintenance events should preserve asset lineage and value/cost effects.

## Logistics and movement

Vehicles, shipments, routes, custody and delivery are represented through existing relationship, event, state, evidence and value contracts.

Physical movement does not create a separate logistics ontology.

## Privacy and sensing

Location, biometric, audio, video, proximity and environmental sensing may involve sensitive data.

Collection and use require the applicable:
- identity;
- authority;
- consent;
- purpose;
- disclosure;
- retention;
- security;
- jurisdiction

controls.

Sensing is not surveillance by default.

## Agent and robot orchestration

ABBA may coordinate physical capabilities through the existing capability fabric and product adapters.

A robot, machine or physical agent cannot acquire broader authority merely because it can execute an instruction.

## Continuity and failure

Physical-world integrations must preserve:
- last-known state;
- uncertain/stale readings;
- pending commands;
- command acknowledgements;
- partial execution;
- safety incidents;
- provider/device failure;
- recovery state;
- handoff/replacement path.

Failures become evidence/state, not silent success.

## Invariants

1. Device capability is not authority.
2. Sensor observation is not automatically truth.
3. Simulation is not observed reality.
4. Physical possession is not ownership.
5. Location is not permission.
6. Automation is not authority.
7. Physical execution remains attributable.
8. Hardware/provider choice remains replaceable.
9. Unknown physical entities remain provisional.
10. Physical failure must remain reconstructable where evidence permits.
