# Carbon Actual Communication & Presence Fabric

**Status:** CANONICAL CAPABILITY CONTRACT  
**Authority:** HAPI World `CANON.md` + Carbon Actual operating-spine contracts  
**Role:** Shared communication, presence, session and representation capability consumed by products and agents.

## 1. Purpose

Communication is an ecosystem capability, not a product-owned silo.

The Communication & Presence Fabric (CPF) provides reusable contracts for messages, media, sessions, presence, participant roles, representations, disclosure, delivery, evidence, continuity and cross-protocol interaction.

CPF does **not** create:
- a second identity system;
- a second authority system;
- a second event/history system;
- a second memory/knowledge source;
- a second orchestration intelligence;
- a product-specific communication constitution.

The rule is:

`one canonical communication meaning → many modalities, transports, providers and product surfaces`

## 2. Canonical boundary

The nine-facet Carbon Actual kernel remains unchanged:

`IDENTITY → AUTHORITY → INTENT → CAPABILITY → RELATIONSHIP → EVENT → EVIDENCE → STATE → VALUE`

Communication and Presence are cross-cutting ecosystem denominators and capabilities expressed through those facets.

Examples:
- Identity identifies who/what is represented.
- Authority determines who may communicate, record, disclose, invite, moderate or act.
- Intent describes why communication occurs.
- Capability describes available modalities, transports and actions.
- Relationship connects participants, representatives, agents and organizations.
- Event records messages, calls, attendance, delivery, decisions and changes.
- Evidence supports transcripts, recordings, attestations and provenance.
- State describes active sessions, presence, delivery, availability and continuity.
- Value captures communication-derived economic, social, informational, time, risk and liability effects.

## 3. Universal communication object

A communication object may represent:
- message;
- document;
- image;
- audio;
- speech;
- video;
- live call;
- meeting;
- room;
- event presentation;
- media artifact;
- transcript;
- caption;
- translation;
- notification;
- invitation;
- decision/minute;
- question/answer;
- action item;
- presence signal;
- status/availability;
- representation;
- communication session.

A communication object is not automatically authoritative merely because it was transmitted or stored.

## 4. Identity and representation

CPF MUST distinguish:
`identity ≠ representation ≠ appearance`

Representations may include:
- real face;
- real voice;
- avatar;
- stylized/anime character;
- synthetic voice;
- synthetic video;
- delegated agent representation;
- cloned/synthetic representation.

Where a representation could reasonably be mistaken for a person, system or organization, provenance and authorization metadata must remain available according to applicable policy.

Synthetic, modified or AI-generated media remain data/evidence until validated by the receiving policy boundary.

## 5. Participant and session model

A session may contain:
- principal;
- participants;
- representatives/delegates;
- agents;
- roles;
- invitations;
- agenda;
- capabilities;
- communication channels;
- recording/transcription policy;
- disclosure policy;
- moderation policy;
- start/end state;
- attendance/presence state;
- evidence/media artifacts;
- decisions;
- actions/tasks;
- continuity references.

Presence is a signal about availability, participation or observed state. It is not proof of identity, intent, consent or authority.

## 6. Communication grammar

Where consequential communication is involved, CPF uses:

`intent → identity/context → authority → capability → policy → send/receive → acknowledgement → event/evidence → verification → state → value/continuity`

Authentication does not equal transaction approval. Delivery does not equal comprehension. Read status does not equal consent. A provider acknowledgement does not equal successful business outcome.

## 8. Transport neutrality

CPF treats transports as replaceable adapters.

Possible transports include:
- IP/internet;
- cellular;
- Wi-Fi;
- Bluetooth;
- device-to-device;
- satellite/direct-to-device;
- radio;
- optical/light;
- acoustic;
- underwater;
- store-and-forward;
- delay/disruption-tolerant;
- local/offline;
- event/API/protocol transport.

The transport is not the canonical communication meaning.

## 9. Privacy and visibility

Communication MUST support explicit visibility/disclosure policies such as:
`public | private | restricted | invite-only | anonymous | pseudonymous | incognito | selective-disclosure | ephemeral`

Tracking is not a default entitlement.

Presence, delivery, read, location, proximity, attendance, recording and telemetry signals require the applicable consent, legal, safety and policy gates.

Metadata minimization and anti-tracking modes are supported capabilities.

## 10. Security and abuse resistance

CPF MUST protect against:
- impersonation;
- deepfake/representation abuse;
- unauthorized session access;
- message tampering;
- replay and duplication;
- malicious links/files/media;
- prompt/tool injection through communication content;
- unauthorized recording;
- covert or abusive tracking;
- harassment and abuse;
- disclosure beyond authorized scope;
- provider compromise;
- continuity loss.

Untrusted content remains data until explicitly promoted through a governed interpretation boundary.

## 11. Provenance and evidence

Material communication artifacts may carry:
- source;
- creator/actor;
- represented principal;
- authority reference;
- timestamp;
- transport/provider;
- content integrity;
- transformation history;
- verification state;
- disclosure class;
- retention state;
- relationship/context;
- evidence links.

Transcripts, summaries and model-generated interpretations are derived artifacts. They must not silently overwrite the underlying communication event or original evidence.

## 12. Meeting and session intelligence

CPF supports reusable session intelligence:
`agenda → invitation → attendance → participation → notes/transcript → questions → decisions → action items → owners/deadlines → evidence → follow-up`

ABBA may orchestrate these capabilities, but ABBA is not the communication system itself.

## 13. Continuity

Communication state must survive material provider/device changes where lawful and technically possible.

Portability should preserve:
- participant identity/context;
- authority/delegation;
- relationship context;
- session state;
- message/media references;
- evidence/provenance;
- commitments/action items;
- retention/deletion state;
- provider dependencies;
- successor/handoff information.

Provider failure must not silently erase canonical continuity.

## 14. Voice and video

Products may expose voice/video surfaces under the ecosystem Voice & Video Surface Law.

CPF owns the reusable capability contract; individual products decide how they present it.

Examples:
- speakable interfaces;
- agent speech;
- live calls;
- meeting rooms;
- media generation/processing;
- transcription;
- captions;
- translation;
- recorded evidence.

## 15. Agent and tool interaction

CPF can represent:
`human → agent`
`agent → agent`
`agent → service`
`service → human`
`machine → machine`

Agent-to-agent or tool communication remains governed by principal identity, authority, capability, policy, evidence and audit context.

## 16. No covert constitutional channel

The fabric does not authorize secret or covert communication intended to evade applicable oversight, consent, law, safety or audit controls.

Privacy-preserving communication is supported; hidden authority is not.

## 17. Maturity and promotion

Capabilities move through:

`candidate → specified → implemented → tested → observed → verified → promoted → monitored → deprecated/replaced`

Experimental and future modalities remain explicitly classified and cannot silently become current capability.

## 18. Composition rule

Products consume CPF.

Examples:
- NOUN Bot consumes communication and presence for education sessions.
- TIP consumes communication for sourcing, negotiation and alerts.
- ZUJID consumes communication for inquiry, professional correspondence and meetings.
- HAPI World consumes communication for AI-native participation and sessions.
- OMNI exposes communication through its operating/integration surface.

Products may add workflow-specific behavior but do not create duplicate universal communication or presence primitives.

## 19. Core invariants

1. Communication is not authority.
2. Presence is not identity.
3. Delivery is not consent.
4. A recording is not automatically truth.
5. A transcript is not the original event.
6. A model summary is not the underlying evidence.
7. A provider is not the constitutional source of communication meaning.
8. A transport is not the communication object.
9. Privacy does not create hidden authority.
10. Historical communication remains reconstructable where evidence permits.
