# Carbon Actual Creative Economy Domain Contract

**Status:** CANONICAL DOMAIN COMPOSITION CONTRACT  
**Implementation status:** Future dedicated product/domain; no current product repository is asserted.  
**Authority:** HAPI World `CANON.md` + Carbon Actual operating-spine contracts

## 1. Purpose

This domain provides reusable architecture for music and broader creative economies without mixing production code into the Nigerian Cultural Atlas.

It covers creation, contribution, rights, production, release, distribution, performance, audience, career, cultural legacy, financing, royalty flows, licensing, events, awards and evidence.

It is a domain composition over existing Carbon Actual contracts, not a new universal ontology.

## 2. Canonical graph

`Artist/Creator → Work → Recording/Media Artifact → Contributors → Rights/Contracts → Release/Distribution → Audience/Performance → Revenue/Royalty → Career/Legacy`

Each edge is represented by existing canonical relationships and events.

## 3. Universal objects reused

The domain uses existing Carbon Actual object and relationship semantics for:
- people, organizations and AI agents;
- works and media artifacts;
- rights and licenses;
- ownership, control and custody;
- contracts and obligations;
- capabilities and capacity;
- opportunities;
- events and sessions;
- evidence and provenance;
- state and lifecycle;
- value, revenue, costs, royalties and settlement.

No separate “music identity,” “creative ledger,” or “artist authority” system is created.

## 4. Creator and contributor model

Creators and contributors may include:
- artist/performer;
- songwriter/composer;
- producer;
- beatmaker;
- engineer;
- mixer/mastering engineer;
- session musician;
- conductor;
- choir/orchestra member;
- choreographer;
- dancer;
- visual creator;
- director;
- videographer;
- editor;
- designer;
- label;
- publisher;
- distributor;
- venue;
- promoter;
- manager;
- educator/coach;
- AI system or agent acting under governed authority.

Contribution is a relationship/event/evidence record, not automatic ownership.

## 5. Work and recording distinction

The domain distinguishes:
- underlying creative work;
- recording/performance;
- arrangement/remix;
- master/media artifact;
- version;
- release;
- derivative/transformed work.

A recording does not erase or replace the rights graph of the underlying work.

AI-generated or AI-modified material carries provenance and authorization context where material.

## 6. Rights graph

The rights layer must distinguish:
`ownership ≠ authorship ≠ performer credit ≠ control ≠ custody ≠ license ≠ assignment ≠ distribution right ≠ synchronization right ≠ performance right ≠ mechanical right ≠ publishing right ≠ royalty interest`

Every material right should carry:
- right type;
- holder/principal;
- scope;
- territory;
- duration;
- exclusivity;
- restrictions;
- transferability;
- evidence;
- contract reference;
- revocation/expiry state;
- revenue/royalty consequences where applicable.

## 7. Split and royalty model

Revenue and royalties remain subordinate to the economic revenue/monetization boundary.

A royalty record should preserve:
- underlying work/recording/right;
- contributor/beneficiary;
- agreed share or basis;
- territory;
- term;
- collection point;
- deductions/fees/taxes where applicable;
- evidence;
- payment/settlement state;
- disputes;
- reversal/adjustment history.

A displayed percentage is not by itself proof of legal entitlement.

## 8. Production and studio capabilities

Reusable capabilities may include:
- studio booking;
- recording;
- editing;
- mixing;
- mastering;
- rehearsal;
- beat/open-beat management;
- remix/version management;
- session musician booking;
- production coordination;
- AI production assistance;
- quality review;
- rights clearance;
- metadata management.

Providers and studios are interchangeable capability implementations.

## 9. Distribution and release

A release may connect:
`work → recording → artwork → metadata → rights → territory → platform/channel → release state → audience → evidence → revenue`

Distribution providers remain adapters.

A provider's catalog state does not become canonical truth without reconciliation.

## 10. Live/events

The shared event and Communication/Presence fabrics cover:
- concerts;
- tours;
- festivals;
- ceremonies;
- rehearsals;
- interviews;
- listening sessions;
- livestreams;
- broadcasts;
- workshops;
- performances;
- auditions;
- awards.

Event records preserve participant roles, attendance/presence, permissions, recording policy, evidence and continuity.

## 11. Awards and reputation

Awards, charts, credentials, public recognition and audience signals are distinct from legal ownership and authority.

A ranking, award, follower count or platform metric is evidence/signaling, not automatic truth of artistic quality, ownership or entitlement.

Fraud, bots, impersonation and manipulated activity must remain observable and challengeable.

## 12. Education and development

The domain may compose InstituteGPT and other capability providers for:
- music education;
- coaching;
- production training;
- performance preparation;
- certification;
- skills verification;
- career development.

Learning records remain subject to the education domain's own authority/evidence contracts.

## 13. Commerce and investment

Creative works and rights may participate in TIP/I/O economic flows where lawful, including:
- licensing;
- advances;
- royalties;
- catalog transactions;
- commissions;
- sponsorship;
- booking;
- ticketing;
- merchandise;
- subscriptions;
- investment;
- financing;
- eligible token representations.

Tokenization remains optional and subordinate to the underlying rights/evidence graph.

## 14. Provenance and synthetic media

Creative media should distinguish:
`human-created | AI-generated | AI-assisted | AI-modified | transformed | unknown`

Material transformations preserve source lineage, contributor context and authorization where available.

The domain may consume content-provenance standards and cryptographic attestations through adapters; standards are implementation inputs, not a competing constitutional authority.

## 15. Annual ABBA 99

The ecosystem may compose an annual 99-contributor creative/cultural project as a governed event/work collection.

Its architecture should preserve:
- contributor identities;
- roles and contributions;
- work/recording lineage;
- rights and splits;
- production evidence;
- release/distribution;
- audience/performance evidence;
- revenue/royalty outcomes;
- cultural legacy/archival continuity.

The project is a composition instance, not a new primitive.

## 16. Cultural Atlas boundary

Nigerian Cultural Atlas remains a cultural publishing/atlas product.

The Creative Economy domain may supply verified cultural/creative objects and events to the Atlas through explicit relationships and publication projections.

The Atlas must not become the production ledger, royalty system, rights registry or studio-management platform for the creative domain.

## 17. Security and authority

Consequential creative actions—rights assignment, licensing, signing, payment, release authorization, account access, representation, publication, deletion or contractual changes—require the applicable principal, authority, policy, evidence and audit controls.

ABBA may orchestrate, but cannot invent rights or authority.

## 18. Future product boundary

No current repository is designated for the Master Music / Creative Economy platform.

When implementation begins, the product must:
- inherit the Carbon Actual kernel;
- consume Communication/Presence;
- consume Economic Revenue/Monetization;
- consume TIP/I/O where applicable;
- consume SPOTIST for discovery;
- consume ABBA for orchestration;
- keep creative-domain code separate from Nigerian Cultural Atlas;
- preserve portable rights/provenance records.

## 19. Invariants

1. A creative work is not automatically an owned asset merely because it exists.
2. Authorship is not identical to every economic right.
3. Credit is not proof of ownership.
4. A platform catalog entry is not canonical rights truth.
5. Revenue is not entitlement.
6. A royalty split is not self-proving.
7. Audience engagement is not legal authority.
8. AI capability does not create ownership or consent.
9. Tokenization does not create rights that do not already exist.
10. Historical/cultural preservation does not replace production/commercial truth.
